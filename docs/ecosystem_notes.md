# Ecosystem Notes

## Study Area

Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve

Location:
Western Ghats, Southern India

States:
- Tamil Nadu
- Kerala
- Karnataka

Area:
Approximately 5,520 square kilometers

Established:
1986

UNESCO Status:
First Biosphere Reserve in India.

---

## Birds

The Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve supports more than 350 bird species, including several endemic and migratory birds. Birds are one of the best bioacoustic indicators because their vocal activity reflects habitat quality, food availability, and biodiversity.

Some commonly occurring species include:
- Asian Koel
- Indian Peafowl
- Black Drongo
- Jungle Babbler
- White-throated Kingfisher
- Red-whiskered Bulbul
- Malabar Whistling Thrush
- Grey Junglefowl
- Indian Paradise Flycatcher
- Rufous Treepie

---

## Frogs

The Western Ghats are one of the world's richest amphibian biodiversity hotspots. Frog calls are highly seasonal and are excellent indicators of water availability and environmental health.

Common frog groups include:
- Bush Frogs
- Tree Frogs
- Indian Bullfrog
- Cricket Frogs
- Skittering Frog
- Malabar Gliding Frog

---

## Insects

Insects produce continuous background sounds that are important components of forest soundscapes.

Important insect groups include:
- Cicadas
- Crickets
- Katydids
- Grasshoppers
- Beetles
- Bees

These insects contribute significantly to the Acoustic Diversity Index.

## Why This Ecosystem?

The Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve is selected because it has high biodiversity, rich bioacoustic activity, and publicly available recordings. Monitoring its soundscape can provide valuable information about ecosystem health and biodiversity conservation.
